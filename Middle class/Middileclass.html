Middle Class

Love, Rain & Second Marriages

Nirmal has a corporate job, a father who cannot sleep, and a wedding to attend where four old friends meet under one bright pandal. Then the rain comes, and Nasha asks him to get wet with her. Over one spring — a blue saree, a rented flat, a highway bus — everyone in this story is trying to be happy without quite knowing how to afford it.

The Reading Place

A window, a lamp, and the spring outside it

Middle Class is a book for one quiet evening. Sit where the light is low, let the window stay open a little, and read it in order — Dhaka in winter, a wedding in spring, then the long bus north. Each chapter is short enough for one sitting and long enough to leave a room behind.

“A hundred and one candles on the sand, and the wind kept putting them out. We kept lighting them.”

Reviews (Rewrite)

Three rewritten notes on the novel — its weather, its women, and the class it is named after.

Reed Books review desk
On the weather

Rain does the work of a whole plot here. It begins as candlelight on a beach and ends as a downpour on a wedding pandal, and every important sentence in between is said while somebody is wet, or about to be. Ahsan writes weather as if it were a relative — familiar, intrusive, impossible to ask to leave.

A novel that smells of wet earth and old houses.

On its women

Netra gives her body and withholds her love; Nasha gives her smiles and withholds her plans; the new mother gives service and then withdraws it. None of them are prizes, and none of them are villains. The book's quiet achievement is that its saddest line — I will never be happy — is spoken by the woman everyone in the room is looking at.

Beauty as labour, and the cost of being looked at.

On the class

Nobody in this novel is poor and nobody is free. There is a flat, a provident fund, a second-hand car and a job that pays fifty thousand; there is also a father dialling strangers from a notebook called Saved Beloved. The middle class of the title is not an income bracket but a waiting room — everyone politely waiting for a life that has already been spent.

A comedy of manners with the manners taken out.

About the book

Middle Class was written in Dhaka between winters — a first novel about a city where everybody has a job, nobody has enough, and love arrives either too early or on a bus that is already late.
