Messrs Jhamela Kini

Trouble Traders Co.

Nilufa has everything a Dhaka winter can offer: a flat in Dhanmondi, a husband who buys diamonds, two yellow birds, and a birthday she spends waiting for the telephone. Suman works late. A famous writer sends jasmine. A girl in the kitchen smiles through a childhood nobody should survive. Between them, quietly, a firm is incorporated — Messrs Jhamela Kini — and every one of them is a partner.

Reviews (Rewrite)

Three rewritten notes on the novel — what it does, where it hurts, and why the title is a joke with a straight face.

Reed Books review desk
On the prose

Ahsan writes the way a room fills with cigarette smoke: nothing dramatic happens for a page, and then you cannot see the window. The domestic detail is exact — the flask of tea, the note in large letters by the telephone, the four cigarettes in seventeen minutes — and it does the work that plot usually has to.

Slow, close, deliberately unglamorous.

On Nilufa

The novel's real subject is not infidelity but the labour of appearing content. Nilufa notices everything and says almost none of it; her forgiveness is not weakness and not nobility, but a decision taken in the dark because the alternative would require a different life. That is a harder portrait than a betrayal plot, and a better one.

A heroine written without either pity or decoration.

On the title

Messrs Jhamela Kini sounds like a trading house and behaves like one. Everyone in the book is buying trouble at a fair price and calling it love, duty, salary or friendship. The joke is never explained, which is why it keeps working after the last page.

A satire that never raises its voice.

From the Author

Amimul Ahsan wrote his first novel in Room 113 of Sir F. Rahman Hall, Dhaka University — three chapters at a friend's flat in Gazipur, the rest back on campus, mostly on a mobile phone. When it was finished he took a rickshaw out with no destination, then sat in the TSC field at night trying to name it.

The name he found was Messrs. Trouble Buy. The novel follows a woman who has everything and still cannot be still; two people standing in front of each other, making a conflict out of what they cannot say. Ahsan writes that it is best read in one sitting. He is a vegetarian, believes in a green digital world, and calls solitude the room where creation happens.
