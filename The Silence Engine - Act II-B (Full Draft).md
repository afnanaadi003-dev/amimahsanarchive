# CHAPTER 25 — DEAD AIR

**EXT. FENWICK STREET — NIGHT**

A part of the city the maps still list and the tax office has forgotten. Boarded shops, a pub with its windows soaped, and at the end of the road a four-storey building with a transmitter mast bolted to the roof and a faded sign: *RADIO FENWICK — 1974–2017.*

The mast is still there. That is the only thing about the building that has been maintained.

**INT. ABANDONED RADIO STATION — STUDIO — CONTINUOUS**

Dust on the faders. A dead ON AIR sign above the door, its red glass gone grey. The acoustic foam on the walls has begun to peel in the shape of a wave.

IRIS comes up the stairs backwards, dragging a flight case. She sets it down, looks at the studio, and exhales — the first full breath she has taken in days.

She opens the case. Three stolen capacitors, a soldering iron, a multimeter, and the brass recorder, laid out on a square of felt like surgical instruments.

ODUYA (60s) is already in the room. He is sitting in the engineer's chair with his hands folded, wearing a cardigan the colour of old tea, and he has been watching her for some time.

IRIS
(startled)
How long have you been there?

ODUYA
Nine years.

IRIS
(a beat)
Right.

ODUYA
You're in my chair.

IRIS
I didn't know it was your chair.

ODUYA
It's the only chair. I've been alone in this building since they switched us to a playlist and forgot to switch us off. I know every sound this place makes. I know the hum the mast makes in the rain. I know the noise the third-floor door makes when someone who shouldn't be here opens it.

He looks at the flight case.

ODUYA (CONT'D)
So. What are we building?

IRIS
We're not building anything.

ODUYA
You've got three capacitors and a face like a woman who's about to do something permanent. What are we building?

A long beat. Iris decides he is going to be here whatever she says.

IRIS
A phase inverter.

ODUYA
(nodding)
That's a brake.

IRIS
That's what I said.

ODUYA
No. You said a phase inverter. I'm telling you what it is. It takes the note and turns it inside out, and if you get the timing wrong it doesn't brake anything — it makes the tower sing.

IRIS
I know what it does.

ODUYA
Do you know what this building does? The stone in the east wall is the old chapel stone. They built the station on the chapel because the chapel had the best acoustics in the district, and the chapel had the best acoustics because it was built on the amplifier. The whole street is standing on the same thing he's using.

Iris stops soldering.

IRIS
How do you know about him?

ODUYA
I've been listening. It's all I've had to do. Nine years of listening to a city that doesn't know it's being tuned.

He stands, crosses to the desk, and picks up a pair of headphones. They are old, enormous, the kind that clamp.

ODUYA (CONT'D)
You can leave. The door's open. I won't call anyone. I've never called anyone.

IRIS
You should go.

ODUYA
(sitting down, putting on the headphones)
No. I want to hear it.

She looks at him for a moment. Then she finishes the last joint, wipes her hands on her trousers, and puts on the second pair of headphones.

She presses the key.

For a second, nothing. Then the transmitter on the roof wakes with a sound like a held breath — a low, rising hum that comes through the floor and into the soles of their feet.

Oduya's face opens. He is not afraid. He looks like a man who has been waiting nine years for the radio to come back on.

**EXT. THE CITY — NIGHT**

On rooftops and in stairwells, in car parks and under bridges, the tone begins to arrive. A hum in the walls. A note in the pipes.

Somewhere, a dog stops barking.

# CHAPTER 26 — THE FIRST CRACK

**EXT. SECTOR 6 — NIGHT**

A residential street of six-storey blocks with washing hanging between them. The counter-tone comes out of every speaker, every phone, every car radio still powered.

People stop.

A man in a dressing gown puts his hand to his chest, the way the woman in the alley told them to. He looks at his own hand as if it has just been returned to him.

MAN
(quietly)
I can — I can hear it again.

His wife comes out of the doorway behind him. She is crying. He turns and holds her, and neither of them can say what has changed, only that something that had been switched off in the middle of their lives has come back on.

Across the street, a woman is standing perfectly still with her hands over her mouth. Her son is tugging at her sleeve. She does not move. She is remembering, all at once, the smell of her mother's kitchen — not the fact of it, but the *feeling* of being small in it — and the size of that feeling is more than her body can hold.

She sits down on the kerb and lets it happen.

**INT. HUB — MAIN FLOOR — CONTINUOUS**

The main screen goes orange. A map of the city with a pulse moving through it, sector by sector.

The fissure in the floor opens another half metre. Dust falls from the ceiling in a thin, steady line, like the building is being measured.

SARA stands at the console, headphones around her neck, watching the orange spread.

SARA
(quietly, to herself)
We're the second button on a coat.

She opens a private file on her tablet. Types: *Log. Thursday. The ward was the control. He said the ward was the control.* Saves it. Hides it in a folder called *Maintenance — Q3.*

VANE comes in. He looks at the orange and does not raise his voice. That is how Sara knows it is bad.

VANE
She's using our amplifier.

SARA
She's using *her* paper.

VANE
(a beat)
Yes. She always was better at the inversion.

He says it with something Sara has never heard from him before and cannot name.

SARA
You're proud of her.

VANE
I'm interested. There's a difference, and it's the only difference that matters in this building.

He walks to the rail and looks down at the array chamber, at the forty emitters sleeping in the stone throat.

SARA
She's going to reach the whole east side by morning.

VANE
I know.

SARA
And then what do we do?

VANE
(not turning)
We let her have the east side. Then we take it back in front of two million people.

Sara looks down at her tablet, at the log she has just written, and closes it before he can see.

# CHAPTER 27 — BROADCAST WAR

**EXT. THE CITY — NIGHT**

Two tones fighting over a skyline.

It is not a battle anyone can see. There are no flashes, no smoke, nothing that a camera could sell. There is only a pressure in the air that changes street by street, and the people in the streets changing with it.

In one road, windows craze in a long line, all at once. In the next, they clear. A woman walking home stops mid-step, turns around, and walks back the way she came with no expression on her face at all.

People fall down. On both sides. Nobody can tell which side they fell for.

**INT. RADIO STATION — STUDIO — CONTINUOUS**

Iris at the desk, headphones on, watching a meter. Her eyes are red. There is a cup of coffee beside her that has been there long enough to grow a skin.

Oduya is at the second desk with a map of the city and a pencil, marking sectors as they report in.

ODUYA
Sector six is holding. Sector four has gone back to gold.

IRIS
Gold?

ODUYA
Their word for it. The ones who like it. They call it the gold. It's on the message boards, it's in the school playgrounds, it's on a banner outside the old cinema.

IRIS
It's feedback. There's nothing gold about a feedback loop.

ODUYA
(quietly)
There's nothing gold about any of it. Doesn't stop people painting it.

The studio door opens. OSEI is in the doorway with two uniforms behind her and her warrant card already out.

OSEI
Iris.

IRIS
(not turning)
I'm a little busy.

OSEI
Turn it off.

IRIS
If I turn it off, Ward Nine stays empty forever. Twenty people in a room with a nurse who writes *unknown* on the chart because there's no other word for what he did to them.

OSEI
If you don't, I have to arrest you for the thing I'm going to arrest you for later anyway, and I'd rather do it when this is finished.

Iris takes off the headphones. The silence in the room is sudden and enormous.

IRIS
How did you find me?

OSEI
I'm a detective. Also, your transmitter is the only thing in this district drawing power.

IRIS
(a beat)
Give me three days.

OSEI
Two.

IRIS
Three.

OSEI
(a long beat)
Two. And I want the name of everyone who helped you. Not for the file. For me.

Iris looks at Oduya. Oduya looks back with the calm of a man who has already decided what he is willing to lose.

IRIS
His name is Oduya. He's the engineer.

OSEI
(to Oduya)
You understand what she's doing?

ODUYA
I've understood for nine years. I've just been waiting for someone to do it on the right frequency.

Osei looks at the two of them, and something in her face gives way — not approval, something older and more tired.

OSEI
Two days. Then I come back with a warrant, and I bring coffee, and we do this the boring way.

She leaves. The uniforms follow.

Iris puts the headphones back on.

# CHAPTER 28 — THE HYMN

**INT. NINTH HOUSE — CHAPEL — NIGHT**

Sixty lay-brothers in grey, standing in the pews. Valerius at the front, in his robes, a phone held up in one hand.

The phone plays Iris's counter-tone. In this room — with its old stone, its high ceiling, its four hundred years of sung prayer — the tone does something it does not do in the street. It sounds like a hymn.

Valerius lets it play to the end. Then he lowers the phone.

VALERIUS
This is the note the machine cannot answer. It is hers, and she made it to break his, and she was right. He cannot answer it.

He looks along the rows of faces.

VALERIUS (CONT'D)
You will carry it into the square. You will not raise a hand. You will not carry a weapon. You will *sing*, and the machine will break itself against you.

FIRST BROTHER
And the woman?

VALERIUS
Bring her to me alive. I want to know what she thinks she has done.

SECOND BROTHER
And if she won't come?

VALERIUS
(a beat)
Then bring me the thing she loves and ask her again.

The brothers do not react. They have been in this house long enough that the sentence does not surprise them. That is what frightens Valerius about his own voice.

**INT. RADIO STATION — STUDIO — CONTINUOUS**

Iris's meter spikes. Oduya pulls off his headphones.

ODUYA
Someone's repeating us.

IRIS
How many?

ODUYA
Sixty. Maybe more. They've got the tone *and* they've got lungs.

Iris is already at the window. In the street below, a line of grey coats is walking toward the station, singing. The sound arrives a second after the sight of them, and it is beautiful. That is the worst part.

IRIS
(standing up fast)
Shut it down.

ODUYA
It's not ours anymore. It's in the air.

IRIS
Then pull the transmitter.

ODUYA
If we pull it now, sector six goes dark with it.

IRIS
*Pull it.*

He reaches for the breaker. Then the singing stops.

Iris looks down. The grey coats have stopped walking. They are standing in the road, heads up, listening to something that is not coming from the station.

Her phone rings. The screen says: *UNKNOWN.*

She answers. Vane's voice, calm, close.

VANE (V.O.)
You should hear this.

He holds the phone up. On the other end, faintly, through the city's noise, there is a third tone. Something between the two of them. Something that has not been there before.

VANE (V.O., CONT'D)
That's the bishop's choir. They're not singing your note. They're singing *his*. And they've tuned it to yours.

IRIS
(quietly)
That's not possible.

VANE (V.O.)
It's happening. Which means in about four hours there'll be a frequency in this city that nobody has ever heard, and neither of us will be the one who chose it.

He hangs up.

# CHAPTER 29 — THE ADDICTION

**EXT. THE SQUARE — NIGHT**

A ring of young men and women, arms out, heads back, singing Iris's note. Their eyes are rolled, their mouths are open, they are *rapturous*.

Around them, a crowd that has come to watch and stayed to join. Someone has brought a speaker. Someone else has brought a generator. The ring is getting bigger.

One of them — a girl of nineteen in a silver jacket, laughing — folds at the knees and hits the pavement without putting her hands out.

The singing does not stop.

Iris pushes through the crowd and kneels beside her. She turns the girl's head gently, checks her breathing, puts two fingers against her throat.

IRIS
Hey. Hey. Look at me. What's your name?

GIRL
(smiling, blissful, slurring)
Doesn't matter. It's so *loud.*

IRIS
(horrified)
She's high.

OSEI
(behind her)
On what?

IRIS
On the same thing he's selling, from the other end. I built a church, Adjoa. I built a *better* church. It gets them in nine seconds instead of thirty years.

She stands up. Her hands are shaking.

IRIS (CONT'D)
We have to stop the broadcast. Ours. All of it.

OSEI
You said it was the only thing holding sector six.

IRIS
It is. And it's also this.

A beat. The girl on the ground is humming the note with her eyes closed.

IRIS (CONT'D)
There's no clean note. There's no clean note anywhere in this.

OSEI
Then what do we do?

IRIS
(watching the ring)
We find the man who started it. And we make him stop in front of everyone he's been lying to.

**INT. THE SQUARE — LATER**

The crowd has thinned. The girl in the silver jacket is sitting up now, wrapped in someone's coat, drinking water, embarrassed.

Iris crouches in front of her.

IRIS
Do you remember what you felt?

GIRL
(a beat)
I felt like everything was going to be all right.

IRIS
And now?

GIRL
Now I feel like I want it again.

Iris stands. She looks at Osei, and Osei looks away first.

# CHAPTER 30 — SARRAF

**INT. SARRAF'S SHOP — NIGHT**

The sign half-fallen in the wind. The door open.

The shop is exactly as it was — the counter, the instruments, the smell of brass and old wood — except that the test rig at the back is running. The meter is pinned. The little speaker on the shelf is putting out a thin, unbroken tone.

Sarraf is in his chair, headphones still on, one hand on the bench, the other in his lap. His eyes are open.

Iris turns the rig off. The silence is enormous.

IRIS
Halim.

She crosses to him. She checks his neck. Then she stands still for a moment with her hand on his shoulder, and then she looks at the rig.

The settings are wrong. Deliberately wrong. The drive is at maximum and the limiter is *bypassed* — and the bypass is not something you do by accident on this machine. It is a set of three switches under a flap, and the flap has to be unscrewed.

Iris unscrews it with a coin. The screws turn easily. They have been turned recently.

On the bench, a printed page. A court summons. *Witness: Sarraf, H.*

IRIS
(to the room)
They didn't want a witness.

She looks at his hand. There is a small burn on the inside of his thumb, the kind you get from a hot valve. He had been trying to reach the switch.

On the floor by the door: a grey thread. A coat button.

**INT. SARRAF'S SHOP — LATER**

Osei photographs the button. Iris sits on the floor with her back against the counter, the summons in her hand.

OSEI
I'll need you to say it out loud for the record.

IRIS
The Order killed my co-author to stop him testifying.

OSEI
Say it again, looking at me.

IRIS
(looking at her)
The Order killed my co-author to stop him testifying.

OSEI
And the man who did it is in a house with a steel door and forty rifles, and I have a button.

IRIS
Then we do it in public. In the square. In front of two million people and a camera crew.

OSEI
You want to accuse the Bishop at his own centenary.

IRIS
I want to take the Bell out of his hands in front of everyone, and I want him to have to explain the room with the rifles while he's standing next to it.

Osei looks at the button in the evidence bag. Then at Sarraf, still sitting at his bench with his eyes open.

OSEI
All right.

IRIS
(surprised)
All right?

OSEI
I've been a detective for thirty-one years. I've buried two partners and a marriage. I'm not going to spend the rest of my career arresting people for the things the men in the big houses did.

She writes something in her notebook and tears the page out.

OSEI (CONT'D)
That's my personal number. You'll need it before Thursday.

# CHAPTER 31 — NELL

**INT. IRIS'S FLAT — NIGHT**

A small flat above a laundrette. The radiator is on. The window is fogged.

A box of her sister's things, still packed, sits in the corner where it was left nine years ago. Iris opens it for the first time.

A hospital band. Photographs. A hair clip. A revival flyer, 2014: *THE NINTH HOUR HEALING MISSION — BRING THE SICK.*

A receipt: *Relic fragment, IX. £400.* And a second, smaller: *£300.*

**FLASHBACK — INT. REVIVAL TENT — 2014**

A marquee on a wet field. Hundreds of folding chairs, half of them full. The air is thick with breath and perfume and the smell of wet grass coming up through the flooring.

NELL (22) in the front row. Thin. Radiant. Wearing a dress she has pressed herself.

A HEALER on the stage with a microphone and a good watch, walking the front row like a man inspecting a fence.

HEALER
There is a sickness in this girl that no doctor has a name for. But the Bell has a name for it.

He puts the little brass fragment in her hand.

HEALER (CONT'D)
Four hundred for the fragment. Three for the laying-on.

Nell looks at the fragment. She is crying. She is smiling. She has been ill for two years and no one has told her she is going to be all right.

Iris, twenty-six, at the back of the tent, is already standing up.

IRIS (V.O.)
I took her home that night. I told her it was a scam. I was right, and I was so pleased with being right that I didn't notice she'd stopped eating.

**END FLASHBACK**

**INT. IRIS'S FLAT — CONTINUOUS**

Iris puts the receipt down.

IRIS (V.O.)
She died fourteen months later. The inquest said malnutrition. The Order sent flowers and a mass card and a receipt for the mass.

She picks up the brass fragment. It is worn smooth on one face. On the base, nearly worn away: IX.

IRIS (V.O., CONT'D)
I've spent nine years wanting to burn that house down. And now there's a man who can actually do it, and he's worse than they are, and I have to be the one who stops him — and I have to do it *without* saving them.

She puts the fragment around her neck, next to the recorder.

Then she goes to the window, wipes a circle in the fog, and looks out at the city. Somewhere out there, the note is moving through the streets.

# CHAPTER 32 — THE SALE

**INT. HUB — CORRIDOR — NIGHT**

Kane in a corridor, phone to his ear. The white light of the hub makes everyone in it look like a man in a hospital photograph.

VANE (V.O.)
You've been meeting her.

KANE
Yes.

VANE (V.O.)
Where is she?

KANE
(a long pause)
The radio station on Fenwick. Third floor. She's got a phase inverter and about six hours of transmitter left.

VANE (V.O.)
Thank you.

Kane hangs up. He stands in the corridor with the phone in his hand.

SARA
(from behind him)
Why.

KANE
(not turning)
Because he'll trade me Ward Nine for her. All twenty of them, moved to a quiet unit with a garden and a nurse who doesn't fill in forms.

SARA
And you believe him.

KANE
No.

A beat.

KANE (CONT'D)
But the kid will get a garden.

He walks away. Sara watches him go, then takes out her tablet and opens her private log.

**INT. WARD NINE — NIGHT**

Kane stands at the door with his visitor's badge and looks in.

Twenty beds. Twenty people. A woman of forty is drawing the same bird on a pad of paper on her knees, over and over, with great care. A boy of six is sitting on his bed with his hands in his lap, smiling at nothing.

KANE (V.O.)
I carried the emitters up those stairs myself. Four trips. The kid was in the corridor on the second trip, and I told him to go back to bed.

He watches the boy for a moment.

KANE (V.O., CONT'D)
He went back to bed.

**INT. HUB — CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS**

Sara types: *He's selling her. Thursday. The ward is the price.*

Then she deletes it, and types instead: *The ward was the control. He said the ward was the control.*

She saves the file and closes the tablet.

# CHAPTER 33 — THE HUB

**EXT. FENWICK STREET — NIGHT**

Four vans. No sirens. Men in grey coats going up the fire escape while Iris is still on air.

**INT. RADIO STATION — STUDIO — CONTINUOUS**

Oduya sees them first. He takes off his headphones, walks to the door, and stands in it.

ODUYA
You'll have to move me. I'm seventy and I've got a bad hip, so it'll take a while.

They move him. Gently, which is somehow worse. Two men take an arm each and lift him aside like a piece of furniture, and one of them says *sorry, sir* as he does it.

Iris doesn't run. She turns the transmitter up, takes the brass recorder out of the desk, and puts it in her coat.

IRIS
(into the mic, live)
This is Iris Kalu. If anyone is still listening — the tone you're singing is mine, and I'm sorry, and you should stop.

The door opens behind her.

**INT. HUB — INTAKE ROOM — LATER**

White walls. A metal table. A chair with no arms.

Iris sits with her hands in her lap. They have taken the recorder and the fragment, but they have not taken her coat, which is a mistake she intends to make use of.

The door opens. Vane comes in alone.

He sits down opposite her and puts a paper cup of water on the table between them.

VANE
I'd like to say I'm sorry about Sarraf.

IRIS
You'd like to say it.

VANE
(quietly)
Yes.

IRIS
Then say it.

VANE
(a beat)
I'm sorry about Sarraf.

IRIS
You didn't do it.

VANE
No. But I made the world where it was a reasonable thing to do. That's not nothing.

Iris looks at him for a long time. It is the first time in years she has been in a room with him without wanting to leave it.

IRIS
You look tired, Elias.

VANE
I am.

IRIS
Good.

# CHAPTER 34 — THE OFFER

**INT. THE HUB — VANE'S OFFICE — NIGHT**

Spotless. Aluminium and white light. It smells like the inside of a hospital.

Iris in a chair. Not restrained. A cup of tea in front of her that she doesn't touch.

Vane sits opposite, sleeves rolled, glasses off.

VANE
You cost me eleven days and a floor.

IRIS
You cost nine people a chapel.

VANE
Fair.

He leans back.

VANE (CONT'D)
Run it with me.

IRIS
(a beat)
What?

VANE
The project. Not the ward, not the control — the *end state.* You're the only person alive who could make it safe. I've been running your paper with your caveats torn out for four years and I know exactly where I'm guessing.

IRIS
You want me to be your conscience.

VANE
I want you to be my *engineer.* A conscience is a thing you have when you can still feel the weight. I can't.

A beat. He says the next part without any self-pity at all, which is what makes it land.

VANE (CONT'D)
You can. That's the whole difference between us and it always was.

IRIS
You put a machine in a children's ward.

VANE
And you built a church in a basement and got a girl of nineteen to fall down in the street. We're the same animal, Iris. I just got there first and I'm less sentimental about the invoice.

Iris looks at her tea. Then she looks up.

IRIS
All right.

Vane doesn't smile. He looks, for a second, almost relieved.

VANE
Thank you.

IRIS
I want the master console. I want the fail-safe left in. And I want to see the whole array before I touch anything.

VANE
(standing)
Of course. You'd be an idiot not to ask.

He goes to the door.

VANE (CONT'D)
I should tell you — the Bell is coming here. Thursday. They're moving it for the centenary and I've made sure it comes through this building.

Iris keeps her face still.

IRIS
Why?

VANE
Because it's four hundred years of the exact thing I'm trying to delete, and I'd like to watch it come apart in the same room as the machine that does it.

He leaves.

Iris sits alone with her tea going cold. Then she reaches into her coat, takes out the brass recorder, and holds it under the table, thumb on the seal.

**INT. THE HUB — CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS**

Vane walks away from his office. His hands are shaking. He puts them in his pockets.

KANE is waiting at the end of the corridor.

KANE
She said yes.

VANE
She said yes.

KANE
Do you believe her?

VANE
(a beat)
No. But it doesn't matter. She'll do the work because she can't stand to see it done badly. That's not the same as trusting her. It's better.

# CHAPTER 35 — THE TRUE TARGET

**INT. HUB — ARCHIVE ROOM — NIGHT**

Iris alone with a terminal. The room is cold. The servers hum.

She pulls the deployment file.

**ON SCREEN:** *OPERATION NINTH HOUR. Target: the Order's centenary pilgrimage. Estimated attendance: 2,100,000. Array: the Bell itself. Duration: 40 minutes. Projected outcome: permanent.*

She scrolls. A note in Vane's hand, scanned:

*"The machine is not the source. It never was. The crowd is. Two million people in one square, one note, and the stone does the rest."*

IRIS
(whispering)
Forty minutes. Two million. Permanent.

She sits back. Then she starts copying the file to a drive in her sleeve — and stops, because Sara is standing in the doorway.

SARA
Don't bother. I've had it for a week.

IRIS
(a beat)
Why haven't you used it?

SARA
Because the last person who was going to testify got his limiter bypassed.

She comes in and sits down.

SARA (CONT'D)
I've written a log. Every Thursday, every number, every time he said the ward was the control. It's in a locker at the swimming pool on Calder Street, and my sister has the key, and my sister thinks I'm a monster.

IRIS
Does she?

SARA
(a small, terrible smile)
I keep the machine running. What else is she supposed to think.

Iris looks at her. Then she writes something on a scrap of paper and slides it across.

IRIS
That's the number of a detective. Adjoa Osei. She's been waiting for someone like you.

Sara picks it up.

SARA
If I do this, I don't work again.

IRIS
If you don't, twenty people in Ward Nine never work again either.

Sara folds the paper and puts it in her pocket.

SARA
(standing)
I've been telling myself that for a year. It doesn't get easier to hear.

IRIS
It isn't meant to.

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — LATER**

Iris stands at the rail above the stone throat. Below her, the forty emitters are dark. The Bell's cradle is empty, waiting.

VANE comes to stand beside her.

VANE
You've seen it.

IRIS
I've seen it.

VANE
And?

IRIS
And it works.

She does not look at him.

IRIS (CONT'D)
That's the problem.

# CHAPTER 36 — THE ANTENNA

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — NIGHT**

The array: forty emitters in a stone throat, bolted to four-hundred-year-old masonry. The Bell's cradle, empty, waiting.

Iris at the console. Leo beside her in a grey tunic, holding a coil of cable.

SARA stands at the far console with her arms folded.

IRIS
I need to trace the source before I can cut it. That means running the tone and reading where it comes back from.

SARA
That means running the tone on somebody.

IRIS
On me.

LEO
No.

IRIS
Leo—

LEO
You'll go under and you won't come back up, because you've got more in your head than anyone and it'll take longer to get out. I've got nothing. That's the one thing I'm good for now.

IRIS
That is not true.

LEO
(patiently)
I know what I've lost. You keep telling me what I haven't. It's the same arithmetic.

Silence. Sara looks at the floor.

IRIS
(very quietly)
Twenty seconds. Then I pull you.

LEO
Forty.

IRIS
Twenty-five.

LEO
(nodding)
Twenty-five.

He sits in the chair. She fits the leads. He looks up at the stone throat, at the emitters, and he smiles — a real one, the first we have seen from him.

LEO (CONT'D)
It's warm in here.

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — MOMENTS LATER**

Vane appears at the rail above with two men. He does not stop it. He watches with his hands behind his back, the way a man watches a river.

IRIS
(into the mic)
Twenty-five seconds. Mark.

The tone comes. Leo's hands open.

On the screen, the trace begins — a line running out from the array, into the city, and *back*, thicker, from everywhere.

SARA
It's not the machine. The return is bigger than the output.

IRIS
(watching)
It's the crowd. They're the amplifier. It was never the emitters — the emitters just start it.

LEO
(from the chair, dreamy)
It's so loud.

IRIS
Eight seconds. Leo. Leo, come back.

LEO
I can hear all of them.

IRIS
Cut it. Cut it now.

SARA
If we cut at this amplitude—

IRIS
CUT IT.

Sara cuts it. The silence arrives like a wall.

Leo is still smiling. His hands are still open.

IRIS (CONT'D)
Leo?

She checks his neck. Then she puts her forehead against his and stays there while Sara cries quietly at the console and Vane, above, writes something down.

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — LATER**

Iris stands up. Her face is wet. She wipes it once, with the back of her hand, the way you wipe rain off a window.

She turns to the console. The trace is still on the screen: the city, the crowd, the return. A map of two million people who have no idea what they are about to be used for.

IRIS
(quietly, to herself)
It was never the Bell.

She starts typing.

Behind her, Vane comes down the steps. He stops a few metres away. He does not offer condolences. He looks at the screen.

VANE
You found it.

IRIS
(not turning)
I found it.

VANE
Then we're ready.

IRIS
(typing)
No. *I'm* ready.

For the first time, Vane looks uncertain.

VANE
Iris—

IRIS
(turning)
You wanted an engineer. You've got one. Now get out of my array.

He holds her eyes for a long moment. Then he nods, once, and goes back up the steps.

Iris turns back to the screen. Her hands are steady now.

IRIS (CONT'D, to the empty room)
Forty minutes. Two million people. One note.

She looks at Leo.

IRIS (CONT'D)
Not on my shift.

**FADE OUT.**
