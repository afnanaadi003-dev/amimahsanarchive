# THE SILENCE ENGINE

**A screenplay in 47 chapters**

Logline: When a disgraced acoustics engineer turns a forgotten research paper into a weapon that strips the meaning out of human belief, the only person who can find him is the woman who wrote that paper — and the only way to stop him is to admit she once wanted him to succeed.

---

**CHARACTERS**

IRIS KALU (38) — acoustic forensics. Left the Order at nineteen. Her sister Nell died after a faith-healing revival sold her a fragment of the Bell.
ELIAS VANE (44) — acoustician. Iris's former colleague. Cannot feel the thing he is deleting.
MAREK KANE (36) — ex-combat engineer. Asks the question nobody else keeps asking.
BISHOP VALERIUS (67) — keeper of the Ninth Bell. Holy, and complicit.
SISTER AOIFE BRENNAN (31) — the Order's archivist. Opens the door.
DET. ADJOA OSEI (54) — homicide. Wants a name, not a movement.
DR. HALIM SARRAF (63) — Iris's co-author. The crack in the whole thing.
SARA LINDQVIST (28) — Vane's systems technician. Keeps a private log.
LEO (19) — a boy who can still hear a note nobody is playing.

---

# CHAPTER 1 — THE ARCHIVE

**INT. THE ARCHIVE — NIGHT**

A concrete cube, no windows, one failing halogen. On an oak table: a 14th-century prayer book, and a black cylinder the size of a flashlight.

ELIAS VANE (44) turns the cylinder over like an egg. MAREK KANE (36) cleans a knife he has not used.

KANE
They've moved the Bell.

VANE
He's sixty-seven and he thinks gold is a kind of medicine. He'll be where the gold is.

Kane opens the book. A name in brown ink inside the cover.

KANE
Someone's grandmother.

VANE
Her name was Miren. She carried it for sixty years because it kept her daughter alive.

KANE
Did it?

VANE
No.

He flips the switch. A hum enters the teeth. The pages lift and hang, trembling, read by something invisible and fast.

Vane brings his hand down. The parchment does not burn or tear — it lets go, sifting into grey powder over his shoes.

VANE (CONT'D)
It's not a weapon, it's a key. The weapon is whatever the mind does when the key turns.

KANE
So this is about a girl.

VANE
It's about a mechanism.

He slides a folded plan across: the inner sanctum, the vault, a resonance node in the foundation.

VANE (CONT'D)
The Order built a bell tower on a natural amplifier and never noticed. We don't have to break in. We have to sing at the right note.

KANE
Why read her name out loud?

VANE
Because somebody should. Once. Before.

The halogen dies. In the dark the grey heap seems to breathe.

# CHAPTER 2 — THE CHAPEL OF THE NINTH HOUR

**EXT. LOW QUARTER — DAWN**

Rain sideways. A chapel wedged between a laundry and a shuttered pharmacy. Three police vans. Nobody hurrying.

**INT. CHAPEL — CONTINUOUS**

Nine people sit in the pews as though the service has reached a long pause. Coats on. Two still hold hymnals. All alive, all breathing, all smiling at nothing.

DET. ADJOA OSEI (54) holds a coffee she has forgotten.

IRIS KALU (38) enters with two hard cases. She listens before she looks. The room is acoustically dead — old stone should answer itself; this air is flat, like a room with the furniture taken out.

IRIS
Did he touch anyone?

OSEI
The priest? Says he stood at the back, prayed, then went outside and was sick.

Iris walks the aisle.

IRIS
Don't look at their faces. Look at their hands.

Every pair of hands is perfectly still. Resting, like tools put down at the end of a shift.

OSEI
No wounds, no gas, no pills, no forced entry. Toxicology comes back clean and I write *unknown* nine times.

IRIS
The rain stopped an hour before they were found.

OSEI
How did you—

IRIS
East window. That scalloping on the plaster isn't water damage. It's standing-wave scarring. Someone played this building like an instrument.

She holds up a shotgun mic.

IRIS (CONT'D)
Empty room, eleven minutes, nobody slams the door.

# CHAPTER 3 — THE FINGERPRINT

**INT. SOUND LAB — DAY**

Foam wedges, two monitors, one good. Iris runs the chapel recording.

A band at 7.83 hertz that should not be there. A harmonic at 18.6 that refuses to behave like a harmonic.

IRIS
(muttering)
You didn't just play them. You *tuned* them.

She opens a six-year-old file.

**ON SCREEN:** *Resonant Desensitisation of Ritual-Affect Circuits. Kalu, I. & Sarraf, H. 2016.*

*"At sufficient amplitude the affect is not suppressed; it is un-indexed — the subject keeps the memory of significance while losing the capacity to assign it. No human trials."*

She drops both waveforms onto one axis.

They match exactly — including a small deliberate error in the third band she made at four in the morning and never fixed.

Her own signature. In her own hand. On nine strangers.

IRIS
(to nobody)
Halim.

**INT. SARRAF'S OFFICE — LATER**

DR. HALIM SARRAF (63) holds a cup he isn't drinking.

IRIS
Say it was stolen.

SARRAF
It wasn't stolen.

IRIS
You gave him the caveats.

SARRAF
He didn't want the caveats.

IRIS
The caveats are what makes it not a weapon. Everything else is a diagram of how to do this to people.

She stops at the door.

IRIS (CONT'D)
There's a deleted clause in his build. He's running sixteen percent, not spec. The manuscript says what happens at sixteen.

SARRAF
I know what it says.

IRIS
Say it out loud, so I know which of us has to stop him.

SARRAF
At sixteen the affect doesn't come back. Ever. The room stays quiet. The person stays in it.

# CHAPTER 4 — THE ORDER

**EXT. THE NINTH HOUSE — DAY**

A stone compound, more fortress than church. Iron gate, four metres. Men in grey coats behind it who are not monks and are not police.

Osei behind Iris with a damp court order.

OSEI
That gets you a room. It doesn't get you the Bishop.

**INT. THE NINTH HOUSE — ARCHIVE — LATER**

SISTER AOIFE BRENNAN (31), ink on her fingers, sets down a box.

IRIS
I want to talk about a locked door.

AOIFE
There's no locked door. There's a *relic.*

IRIS
There's a room behind it with steel in it, and forty men who carry it into a chapel. That's not devotion. That's a garrison.

AOIFE
(a beat)
Yes. It is.

IRIS
(surprised)
You agreed with me.

AOIFE
I've written forty letters about that room and nobody has answered one. The Bishop says the Bell is the last thing standing between this city and something worse, and I'm thirty-one and I have no argument against him that doesn't sound like a child.

IRIS
Try one anyway.

Aoife sits, slowly, as if her legs decided without her.

AOIFE
He'll see you in twenty minutes. He'll be polite. He'll tell you the Order forgives you for leaving, which means he hasn't. Then he'll tell you nothing.

IRIS
And you?

AOIFE
I'll tell you where the armory key is kept. Then I'll go to confession. Then I'll probably help you again.

Iris sets a small brass object on the table: a broken bell without a clapper.

IRIS
What is this?

Aoife picks it up. Her face does something complicated.

AOIFE
Where did you get it?

IRIS
My sister wore it. Four hundred, at a revival. It was supposed to cure her.

Aoife turns it. On the base, worn nearly smooth: IX.

AOIFE
That's ours. That's from the Bell. Someone has been selling fragments of the relic to dying people and calling it medicine.

IRIS
For how long?

AOIFE
Long enough that I stopped being surprised.

# CHAPTER 5 — SARRAF

**INT. SARRAF'S HOUSE — NIGHT**

A flat above a shuttered repair shop: *SARRAF & DAUGHTER — INSTRUMENTS REPAIRED & TUNED.* Iris lets herself in with a key she's had since she was twenty-six.

His laptop wakes under her hand. An email from *e.vane*: *"Amplitude holding at sixteen. The ward is stable. Iris's model was right about the drift, wrong about the recovery window. She'll understand when she sees it."*

She photographs the screen. Sarraf comes in with groceries and stops dead.

IRIS
What ward.

SARRAF
You shouldn't be—

IRIS
You have a key. I have a key. Sit down.

IRIS (CONT'D)
You told me the recovery window was the one good thing in the paper.

SARRAF
It's a hole.

IRIS
Since when?

SARRAF
Since April.

IRIS
And you didn't call me?

SARRAF
I called you eleven times.

She scrolls. There they are. April. The fortnight she spent sorting out Nell's estate and could not stand to hear anyone's voice.

IRIS
I thought you were calling about the funeral.

SARRAF
I was. The first three times.

IRIS
He's going to do the Bell.

SARRAF
He's going to do the *square*. The centenary. Two million people. He's not going to shoot them, he's going to tune them, and they'll walk home and not be able to say why they went.

IRIS
Then we stop him with the other half of the paper.

SARRAF
There is no other half.

IRIS
There's the part we cut. The counter-tone. The reason we said no human trials isn't ethics, Halim. It's that the system *oscillates*. It listens for an answer, and if it gets one, it doesn't know how to stop.

SARRAF
(softly)
That's what he said.

IRIS
Then I'll be better at it than he is.

At the door she stops.

SARRAF
He's not wrong about them. The armory. The brother they moved instead of handing over. He's not wrong about any of it, and that's why he's going to win.

IRIS
I know what they are. I grew up in that house.

(beat)

It was never about whether they deserve it. It's about who decides what a mind is allowed to feel.

# CHAPTER 6 — PROCUREMENT

**EXT. THE SHIPYARDS — NIGHT**

Forty decommissioned sonar emitters in a shipping container, stacked like organ pipes. A scrapyard man called DOSS in a lawn chair with a shotgun across his knees.

DOSS
You want all forty.

KANE
I want all forty.

DOSS
That's a lot of noise for a man with no employer.

Kane hands over a purchase order and a company card. PARRISH ACOUSTIC SURVEY, LTD. A company that was dissolved four years ago, whose director died in the same year.

DOSS
(squinting)
Parrish. Didn't he—

KANE
He's very keen on this project.

DOSS
(loading them)
You know what these do to people? Properly wired?

KANE
They make a room very quiet.

DOSS
They make a room *nothing*. My brother-in-law was on a rig when they tested these. He came home and he couldn't look at his own kid.

KANE
(handing a note)
The rest on delivery. Don't use the phone.

Doss watches him go. Then he looks at the note. It is not money. It is a name and an address.

DOSS (CONT'D)
(to the empty yard)
What the hell is this.

**INT. VAN — CONTINUOUS**

Kane drives. Behind him the emitters shift and clang. He calls.

KANE
Forty, delivered by four. Where do you want them first?

VANE (V.O.)
Ward Nine.

A long pause on Kane's face.

KANE
That's a hospital.

VANE (V.O.)
It's a room with the wrong note in it.

# CHAPTER 7 — AOIFE

**INT. NINTH HOUSE — THE CHAPEL OF THE DEAD — NIGHT**

Nine coffins, all empty. The nine from the chapel are alive. On trestles at the front: their coats, their hymnals, one pair of shoes.

Aoife and Iris stand at the rail.

AOIFE
They say the Bell was rung at a massacre and swallowed the screams. Four hundred years of silence in a lump of gold, and we kneel to it.

IRIS
Do you believe that?

AOIFE
I believe people come here at four in the morning because they can't sleep, and they leave able to sleep. I don't need the Bell to be magic. I need it to be *load-bearing*.

IRIS
And the armory?

AOIFE
Is the reason I stopped taking communion.

She takes a key from around her neck — a plain iron thing — and holds it out.

AOIFE (CONT'D)
I'm not doing this because I've decided you're right. I'm doing it because you're the only person in this city who has told me the truth twice.

IRIS
You don't know me.

AOIFE
I know your sister's name. It's on a list in our bursar's ledger, under *relic fragments: charitable distribution.*

Iris's hand closes around the key.

IRIS
How much did he charge her?

AOIFE
Four hundred. And then three hundred more when it didn't work.

# CHAPTER 8 — RESONANCE

**INT. SOUND LAB — NIGHT**

On the bench: the shattered east window of the chapel, laid out in seven pieces on butcher paper.

Iris taps a shard with a pencil. It rings. She records it. Taps another. Records. Builds a tone from a dead window.

IRIS
(to herself)
You can't lie to glass. It only knows one note.

She plays the reconstructed tone. The waveform resolves: 7.83, 18.6, and a third band, low, waiting.

Then she does the thing she will spend the rest of the film paying for. She inverts it.

The counter-tone appears on the screen, a mirror image, and the room's foam wedges tremble.

OSEI
(from the door, unseen)
What was that.

IRIS
(startled)
How long have you been there?

OSEI
Long enough to hear a room shake. What are you building?

IRIS
A brake.

OSEI
For a car that doesn't exist yet.

IRIS
It exists. It's just not finished.

Osei comes in and looks at the seven shards.

OSEI
Nine people are alive in a hospital ward and can't tell you their own names. I have a mayor who won't return my calls and a Bishop who sent me a fruit basket. And now I have a forensic consultant building something in a basement at midnight.

IRIS
I wrote the paper.

OSEI
(a long beat)
Say that again.

IRIS
In 2016. With Halim Sarraf. The model Vane is running is mine. I put the numbers in the wrong order and somebody read them.

Osei sits down. She looks, for the first time, tired.

OSEI
Then you're going to help me, and afterwards I'm going to arrest you, and neither of us is going to enjoy how that works out.

IRIS
Deal.
# CHAPTER 9 — THE COURTYARD

**EXT. THE SANCTUARY COURTYARD — NIGHT**

Rain on stone. Forty PALADINS in ornate armour around an iron gate, long spears, gold visors. Statues of faith.

Then a sound that is not a scream: a frequency. The air shimmers.

The paladins stagger. They drop their spears. No blood — only a violent loss of equilibrium, men in forty kilos of steel sitting down in the wet like children.

KANE and a squad come out of the perimeter wall in noise-cancelling headsets.

KANE
(comms)
Inner sanctum. Go.

A PALADIN gets to his knees and starts to pray, voice cracking.

An OPERATIVE triggers his emitter at the man's helmet.

The gold plating doesn't dent. It shatters inward.

The paladin falls. The operative crouches beside him, curious, almost kind.

OPERATIVE
Your god isn't picking up the phone.

He does not look up at the tower window, where a light comes on and a curtain twitches.

**INT. NINTH HOUSE — BISHOP'S STUDY — CONTINUOUS**

BISHOP VALERIUS (67) watches from the window, robe half on, a man woken at the wrong hour of his life.

VALERIUS
(to his secretary)
Get everyone out of the courtyard. All of them. Then call the papers before the police.

SECRETARY
The papers, Your Grace?

VALERIUS
Before the police. Yes.

**EXT. COURTYARD — CONTINUOUS**

Kane looks up at the light in the tower window. He does not raise his weapon.

KANE
(comms)
He's watching.

VANE (V.O.)
Let him.

# CHAPTER 10 — AFTERMATH

**EXT. THE SANCTUARY COURTYARD — DAWN**

Police tape. Actual police this time. Iris picks her way across the courtyard past dropped spears still lying where they fell.

The visor victim is under a sheet. Around him, kneeling, four paladins who were not hit — and who cannot get up.

IRIS
What's wrong with them?

OSEI
Nothing. No injury. They just won't stand.

Iris crouches by a paladin. Fifty, grey, hands folded in his lap.

IRIS
Can you hear me?

PALADIN
(politely)
Yes.

IRIS
What's your name?

PALADIN
(a beat)
I know it. I know I know it. It's just not — it's not attached to anything.

IRIS
(quietly)
Tell me about your hands.

PALADIN
(looking down)
They're hands.

IRIS
That's all?

PALADIN
That's all.

Iris stands. She walks away fast, to the wall, and stands with her palm flat against the wet stone.

OSEI
(following)
Iris.

IRIS
They're not hurt, Adjoa. That's the point. They've been *un-indexed*. The memory is there. The meaning isn't.

OSEI
In English.

IRIS
He didn't take their faith. He took the part of them that could care about having it. This is stage two. The chapel was the demonstration. This is the product.

OSEI
And the mayor?

IRIS
(looking at her phone)
The mayor is doing a press conference at nine and he's going to call it a "spontaneous civic awakening."

OSEI
He wouldn't.

IRIS
He's got a re-election in eleven months and a crowd outside his office that thinks this is the best week of their lives. Of course he would.

# CHAPTER 11 — CITY HALL

**EXT. CITY HALL STEPS — DAY**

A crowd of two thousand. Handmade signs: *SILENCE IS A GIFT. THE QUIET IS OURS.*

MAYOR EBERHARDT (58) at a lectern, reading a statement he did not write.

MAYOR
What happened at the sanctuary was not an attack. It was a *clarification*. For the first time in this city's history, nobody is being told what to feel by men in gold hats.

The crowd roars.

Iris stands at the back with Osei, holding a folder.

IRIS
Nine people in a chapel can't say their own names.

OSEI
Say it louder.

IRIS
They'd tear me apart.

OSEI
Then say it in a courtroom. Or don't, and watch him do the cathedral next.

A woman in the crowd turns and looks at Iris. She is holding a child's hand. She is crying, and smiling, and she says the thing that will follow Iris for the rest of the film:

WOMAN
Thank you.

IRIS
(very quietly)
I didn't do anything.

WOMAN
You're the sound woman. Everybody knows. You made it quiet.

# CHAPTER 12 — THE MESSAGE

**INT. SOUND LAB — NIGHT**

A plain brass tape recorder on Iris's desk. She doesn't remember leaving it there. The seal on the back is broken.

She presses play.

VANE (V.O.)
You wrote the theory, Iris. I only built the room. Come and see what it does — the reservoir, Thursday, four in the morning. Bring the brass thing your sister wore. I want you to understand what it was for.

The tape runs on. Thirty seconds of room tone. Then, faintly, a child humming.

Iris plays it again. And again. Then she sits down on the floor with her back against the desk, the recorder in her lap, and stays there until the streetlights go off.

**FADE OUT.**

**END OF ACT ONE.**

# CHAPTER 13 — THE RESERVOIR

**EXT. THE OLD RESERVOIR — 4 A.M.**

A drained concrete tank the size of a cathedral, open to the sky. Twelve emitters on tripods. In the middle, a chair. In the chair, a VOLUNTEER (30s) in a clean shirt.

IRIS comes down the service ladder with her case and a brass object on a cord around her neck.

VANE
You came alone.

IRIS
You asked.

VANE
I asked because I knew it would work.

He is thinner than she remembers. Calmer. He does not look like a man who has done anything wrong; he looks like a man who has finally stopped being interrupted.

VANE (CONT'D)
This is Tomas. He's been in the program for six weeks. He had a — what did you call it, Tomas?

TOMAS
A habit of praying.

VANE
A habit of praying.

IRIS
Get up, Tomas.

TOMAS
(politely)
I'd rather stay.

IRIS
(to Vane)
Turn it on and I'll break every emitter in this room.

VANE
You won't.

He is right. She has already taken out her phone and started recording — a small red light, which he sees, and does not mind.

VANE (CONT'D)
Record it. I want you to have the numbers.

He turns the master switch.

The hum comes down the tank like weather. Tomas's hands open. His face does not change; it *unfocuses*, the way a face does when a conversation ends.

IRIS
Tomas. What did you pray for?

TOMAS
(thinking about it, honestly)
I don't know. Something about a — there was a thing. It was important.

IRIS
What thing?

TOMAS
I can see it. I can't get to it.

Vane turns it off. The silence has edges again.

IRIS
(quiet)
Say the sentence you said to him.

VANE
(mildly)
I said: you won't lose anything. You'll just stop needing it.

Iris closes her eyes. On the phone, the red light keeps recording.

# CHAPTER 14 — WARD NINE

**INT. ST. ALDWYN'S HOSPITAL — WARD NINE — DAY**

Twenty beds. Twenty people sitting up, eating when fed, not eating when not.

A NURSE moves between them with a tray.

NURSE
They're not depressed. Depression you can talk to. These ones answer every question you ask and none of the answers mean anything to them.

IRIS
Show me one who was religious.

NURSE
(pointing)
Bed four. She was a lay reader for thirty years. Now she asks me what a book is for.

Iris sits on the end of bed four. A WOMAN (60s) looks at her pleasantly.

IRIS
What's your favourite hymn?

WOMAN
(politely)
I know the words to several.

IRIS
Sing me one.

The woman opens her mouth. Nothing comes. Not emotion — *motive.* She has the words and no reason to spend them.

IRIS
(standing)
Who authorized this ward?

NURSE
It's not a ward. It's a *recovery cohort*. There's a man comes on Thursdays with a form.

# CHAPTER 15 — KANE'S FIRST DOUBT

**INT. WARD NINE — LATER**

KANE, in a visitor's jacket, stands at the foot of a bed with a clipboard he is not reading.

A BOY (6) sits cross-legged with a crayon, drawing the same picture over and over: a house, a sun, a bird.

KANE
What's that?

BOY
It's my picture.

KANE
What's the bird called?

The boy looks at the bird. He looks at Kane. He looks back at the bird.

BOY
(patiently, as if explaining to a slow adult)
It's the bird in the picture.

Kane crouches. His voice goes quiet.

KANE
Do you like it?

BOY
(thinking)
I draw it every day.

KANE
That's not what I asked.

BOY
(a small frown, the first emotion in the room)
I don't know how to answer that.

Kane stands up. He walks out of the ward and down the corridor and into the stairwell, where he stands with both hands on the railing for a long time.

Then he takes out his phone.

KANE
It's Kane. Put me through to Vane.

A pause.

KANE (CONT'D)
No — tell him the ward is wrong. Not the numbers. The *kid*. Tell him there's a kid in there who can't say if he likes a bird.

He listens. Then he hangs up without saying goodbye.

# CHAPTER 16 — RETALIATION

**EXT. THE HUB — SERVICE ENTRANCE — NIGHT**

A technician called BRENNAN (no relation) comes out for a cigarette. Two men in grey coats are waiting.

**INT. STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS**

They take him up four flights. They don't ask about the weapon. They ask about the *archivist* — who has been seen with a woman from the sound lab.

BRENNAN
I don't know anything.

FIRST BROTHER
You have a key.

BRENNAN
I have a key to a *lift*.

They beat him in a stairwell with the efficiency of men who have done this before and were forgiven for it.

**INT. HOSPITAL — CORRIDOR — LATER**

Iris and Aoife outside the room. Brennan's jaw is wired.

AOIFE
(quiet)
I know them. The one on the left takes the collection at the eight o'clock.

IRIS
And you're still going to tell me the Order is load-bearing.

AOIFE
(flaring, for the first time)
I never said the *men* were. I said the Bell was. Don't make me defend the men, Iris. I've spent my whole life not defending the men.

IRIS
(a beat)
I'm sorry.

AOIFE
Don't be sorry. Be *fast*. They're frightened now, and frightened men in that house do worse than this.
# CHAPTER 17 — THE ARMORY

**INT. NINTH HOUSE — THE ARMORY — NIGHT**

Aoife's iron key. Behind the Bell's display case: a steel door, and behind the door, a room that smells of oil.

Racks. Forty carbines. Body armour. A crate of stun grenades with a municipal inventory tag.

IRIS
Municipal.

AOIFE
They buy from the same depot as the police. It's legal. It's all legal. That's the trick of it.

On the wall: a framed photograph, forty men in grey coats, 2019.

Iris takes out her phone. Aoife puts a hand over it.

AOIFE
Not yet. If you take the picture now, they'll know the key was used and they'll change every lock in the house, and then we have nothing.

IRIS
Then when?

AOIFE
When it can't be undone.

On a desk: a ring binder. Iris opens it. A file, tabbed, with a name: BROTHER CAEL.

IRIS
(reading)
"Transferred: Ninth House to St. Brannock's, 2011. Transferred: St. Brannock's to the Low Quarter Mission, 2014. Transferred: Low Quarter to the coast house, 2017."

AOIFE
(not looking)
Nine houses. Nine years.

IRIS
What did he do?

AOIFE
What do you think he did.

Iris closes the binder. She is very still.

IRIS
And you kept him.

AOIFE
The Bishop kept him. I filed the transfers. I was twenty-three and I typed them and I told myself the file was a kind of justice because at least it *existed.*

(beat)

It wasn't.

# CHAPTER 18 — VALERIUS

**INT. NINTH HOUSE — BISHOP'S STUDY — NIGHT**

A warm room. Books, a fire, a window over the courtyard where men fell.

Valerius pours two glasses of water, because he does not drink, and offers her one.

VALERIUS
You left at nineteen. You wrote me a letter. I still have it.

IRIS
I called your house a machine.

VALERIUS
You called it a machine that eats the young. It was a good letter. I've quoted it.

IRIS
I'm here about a room with forty rifles in it.

VALERIUS
(without flinching)
I know.

IRIS
And about Brother Cael.

Valerius sets his glass down.

VALERIUS
Yes.

IRIS
That's it? Yes?

VALERIUS
What would you like? A denial? I have made three. They were all lies and they all worked, and I stopped being able to say them in 2017.

IRIS
Then why is he still in a house with children in it?

VALERIUS
Because the alternative was the police, and the police meant the papers, and the papers meant the Bell.

(beat)

Nine hundred people come through this house in a week, Iris. They come because they cannot sleep. If the Bell becomes a scandal, it stops being load-bearing, and then they have nowhere to go and nothing to kneel to at four in the morning. I weighed that. I have weighed it every day for nine years.

IRIS
You weighed a child against a lump of gold.

VALERIUS
I weighed a city against a man. I was wrong, and I did it anyway, and I will answer for it. But not to a man who is turning minds off with a machine and calling it hygiene.

(beat)

You want my armory? Take it. I'll open it myself if you can promise me the Bell survives the week ironed out in a courtroom.

IRIS
I can't promise you that.

It's feedback.

SARA
Look at them.

IRIS
It's feedback, Sara. It's four hundred years of a story and two million people standing on a stone amplifier in the rain. There's nothing in it. There's nothing *in* it.

SARA
(quietly)
There is for them.

Iris's hand hovers over the fail-safe.

---

# CHAPTER 43 — THE KILL

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — NIGHT**

Vane at the rail above, hands behind his back, watching the gold like a man watching his own funeral.

VANE
Now.

IRIS
There's a second way. I can bleed it down over six hours and lose the north side of the square to crush injuries.

VANE
There isn't a second way. Six hours is three hundred thousand people hearing a miracle and telling their children about it.

(beat)

Do it, or I'll do it from the console and I'll do it at sixteen.

Iris puts her hand on the key.

IRIS
(into the city's speakers, live)
This is Iris Kalu. I built the machine that is doing this. What you're feeling is a room and a crowd. It isn't a god and it isn't the gold. In eleven seconds it's going to stop, and you're going to hate me, and I want you to know that I know exactly what I'm taking from you.

She presses it.

**EXT. THE SQUARE — CONTINUOUS**

The gold goes out. Not gradually — *instantaneously*, like a switch, and the two million are left standing in the rain in the dark with their hands out.

The grief arrives all at once. A sound comes off the square that is not a scream and not a shout; it is the noise a crowd makes when something dies in front of it.

Then the grief finds a direction.

**INT. HUB — ARRAY CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS**

On the monitors: the crowd turning toward the building. Faces. Bottles. A barrier going over.

SARA
They're coming here.

IRIS
(watching, exhausted)
Of course they are.

---

# CHAPTER 44 — THE DROWNED STAIR

**INT. HUB — UNDERCROFT — NIGHT**

A stone throat under the building, black water to the knee. The old bell foundation, four hundred years deep. Vane ahead of her with the portable array keyed to the Bell's cradle and the Bell's note still in it.

IRIS
Elias.

He doesn't stop. Water slapping stone.

IRIS (CONT'D)
There's nowhere to take it. The Bell knows the square. You ring it anywhere else and it's a very expensive piece of metal.

VANE
It knows the stone. Stone is stone.

IRIS
Two million people were the amplifier. Without them it's a hum and a headache and a court case.

He stops. Turns. In the dark she can just see him.

VANE
Then I'll find two million people.

IRIS
Not tonight you won't. Tonight there's a crowd at the door who want to hang me for taking their miracle and you for making it.

VANE
(a long beat)
I never could feel it. Not once. Four years and I never once felt the thing I was deleting.

IRIS
That's because you were the thing that was missing.

---

# CHAPTER 45 — RESONANCE

**INT. UNDERCROFT — CONTINUOUS**

Iris takes the brass recorder out of her coat — the same unit he left on her desk in chapter twelve. She opens the back.

IRIS
You gave me this. You wanted me to have the numbers.

VANE
I did.

IRIS
You made it out of the prototype casing. The resonator's still inside. It's the same stone you're standing in, Elias. It'll key off the throat and it'll answer you in your own note.

VANE
(steady)
You'll collapse the vault.

IRIS
Probably.

She turns the dial.

The array's hum changes key — slightly, just enough that it stops agreeing with him and starts agreeing with itself. The water begins to move in slow rings.

VANE
I should be afraid.

IRIS
(climbing the steps)
I know.

VANE
(calling after her, almost a question)
Iris. I should be *afraid.*

She doesn't answer. The gold light comes down through the grate and takes the shape of the stone, and the last we see of him is a man standing in a room he has made perfectly quiet, waiting for a feeling that is never going to arrive.

**INT. HUB — MAIN FLOOR — DAWN**

Iris comes up out of the undercroft into a building full of police, and Osei, and Aoife with the ring binder in her arms, and Sara holding a tablet like a shield.

OSEI
Where is he?

IRIS
In the stone.

---

# CHAPTER 46 — HEARINGS

**INT. HEARING ROOM — SIX MONTHS LATER**

A plain room, a long table, a commission.

BISHOP VALERIUS in a grey suit instead of robes, which makes him look smaller and somehow more honest.

CHAIR
The armory. The brother moved from house to house for nine years instead of being handed to police. The fragments of the Bell sold to dying people as medicine.

VALERIUS
I have never denied any of it.

CHAIR
You've never confirmed it either.

VALERIUS
I confirmed it the day I was arrested.

(beat)

I would like the Bell returned to the Order.

CHAIR
The Bell is evidence.

VALERIUS
Then it is evidence that two million people were saved by a thing I was told to be ashamed of.

He looks at Iris, three rows back.

VALERIUS (CONT'D)
You grew up in my house.

IRIS
Yes.

VALERIUS
And you still stopped him.

IRIS
I didn't do it for you.

VALERIUS
(nodding, almost gently)
No. You did it for the same reason I kept the armory locked. Somebody has to decide. We just disagreed about who.

He is led out. She doesn't watch him go.

**INT. HEARING ROOM — LATER**

CHAIR
Ms. Kalu. You built the counter-tone without authorization, broadcast it into a city of four million, and then used it on two million people without consent, medical supervision, or a warrant.

IRIS
Yes.

CHAIR
Do you have anything to say before we consider charges?

IRIS
(taking a breath)
Ward Nine is still there. Twenty people who can't say what their own hands are for. The boy draws the same bird every day. Nobody's been charged for that.

(beat)

Charge me. But put the ward in the same file.

**INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS**

Aoife, in plain clothes now, waits with a paper cup.

AOIFE
They'll go for a fine. Maybe a suspension of your licence.

IRIS
They should go for more.

AOIFE
They will. It'll just take a year and it'll be quieter than you want.

Iris takes the cup.

IRIS
What happens to the house?

AOIFE
The house goes on. The armory doesn't. The Bell goes in a museum with a very long caption, and I go to a different diocese where nobody knows I opened the door.

IRIS
Aoife.

AOIFE
(quietly)
I filed nine transfers. I keep thinking about the nine years.

IRIS
Then don't stop thinking about them.

---

# CHAPTER 47 — A BELL, OFF-KEY

**EXT. THE SQUARE — ONE YEAR LATER — DAWN**

The tower rebuilt. The square repaved, with a circle in the middle where the mandala was, filled in flat.

A small crowd on ordinary business: a market setting up, a dog, a man asleep against a wall.

Then the bell rings. Badly. Deliberately badly — someone up there is pulling the rope out of time, and the note is wrong, and it goes on being wrong.

Iris stands in the middle of the square with her hands in her pockets and listens to the whole ugly thing.

SARA (O.S.)
You know they do that on purpose now.

Iris turns. Sara, with a takeaway coffee and a job in a hospital acoustics lab.

SARA (CONT'D)
It's the ninth hour. Nobody can ring it right. They say it's a penance.

IRIS
It's a rope and a bad bell.

SARA
Yes.

(beat)

You still hear it?

IRIS
(after a moment)
Sometimes. In a lift. In the tunnel under the river.

SARA
What does it sound like?

IRIS
Like the room being very quiet.

The bell rings again, off-key, and the market goes on around them.

IRIS (CONT'D)
I used to think the worst thing you could do to a person was take their god away. It isn't. It's deciding for them what they're allowed to feel about it.

SARA
And the Bell?

IRIS
(looking up at the tower)
The Bell's a lump of gold with a story in it. So is everything. Get it wrong and people die.

(beat)

Get it right and somebody rings it badly on a Tuesday and the whole square stops for a second.

She listens. The note comes again, wrong, human, and she lets herself cry in the middle of a crowd of strangers who have no idea who she is.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

**THE END.**
