TITLE: THE FUNERAL HOME
MIN: 6
@@SLATE
Camera :: A quiet room with a screen and twelve chairs, then a close-up of a shoebox of photographs — the camera learns a face from paper before it meets it on glass.
Roll :: Digital, 2000 ISO, wall sconces and one window with a blind the colour of milk.
Costume :: OWEN TATE in a dark suit that fits, which in this county is a statement. A daughter in her fifties in a cardigan.
Environment :: Tate & Sons Funeral Home: a viewing room, a screen on a stand, a box of tissues on every third chair, a guest book with a pen on a chain.
@@SCENES
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. TATE &amp; SONS — ARRANGEMENT ROOM — MORNING</h4>
      <p class="action">OWEN TATE, thirty-nine, spreads forty photographs on a table like a card trick.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">Eleven minutes. Music at the end. No photos of her in the last two years — the family asked.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">Why the last two years?</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">Because that's what she looked like when she was ill, and they want to remember the other one. It's not a lie. It's a choice about which true thing to put on the wall.</p></div>
      <p class="action">June starts scanning. Owen stands behind her with his hands folded, which is a habit from work and also just who he is.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">You've done this before.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">My father's shop has been in this county for nineteen years. I've done everything before.</p></div>
    </div>
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. TATE &amp; SONS — VIEWING ROOM — AFTERNOON</h4>
      <p class="action">Twelve chairs. Fourteen people. A screen showing a woman in a garden in 1994, holding a hose, laughing at somebody off camera.</p>
      <p class="action">The daughter is in the front row with her hands in her lap, absolutely composed.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="paren">(whispering, at the back)</p><p class="dline">Watch her hands.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">What?</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">Nobody cries with their face first. It's the hands.</p></div>
      <p class="action">On screen, the woman turns and says something to the camera. The daughter's hands come apart.</p>
    </div>

TITLE: ELEVEN MINUTES
MIN: 7
@@SLATE
Camera :: The screen from the back of the room, with the audience's heads in the lower third — the same composition as a church basement, in a different room.
Roll :: Digital, 3200 ISO, the screen is the only light source; the sconces are turned off out of respect.
Costume :: The family in dark clothes. Owen at the door in his suit. June at the projector stand in her fleece, because she is staff.
Environment :: The viewing room at two in the afternoon: twelve chairs, a box of tissues, a photograph of the deceased by the door in a frame that has been reused.
@@SCENES
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. TATE &amp; SONS — VIEWING ROOM — AFTERNOON</h4>
      <p class="action">Eleven minutes. A garden, a kitchen, a bowling alley, a dog that is dead too. Then a photograph that is not of the family at all: a man in a cap on a boat, holding up a fish.</p>
      <p class="action">The room makes a sound. The daughter leans forward.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">DAUGHTER</p><p class="dline">That's Uncle Ray.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">SON</p><p class="paren">(from the second row)</p><p class="dline">That's the wrong uncle.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">DAUGHTER</p><p class="dline">That's Uncle Ray from the boat. Mom kept his picture in the drawer with the batteries.</p></div>
      <p class="action">The room laughs — the good kind, the kind that comes out of a funeral like a bird out of a bush. Owen's shoulders come down an inch.</p>
    </div>
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. TATE &amp; SONS — CORRIDOR — LATER</h4>
      <p class="action">The family has gone. Owen is stacking chairs. June is coiling cable.</p>

      <p class="action">Owen puts a chair against the wall and looks at her.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">That was the best eleven minutes I've shown in that room in five years and I show eleven minutes three times a week.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">It wasn't mine to leave in.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">OWEN</p><p class="dline">No. It was hers. That's why it worked.</p></div>

TITLE: THE MUSIC VIDEO
MIN: 7
@@SLATE
Camera :: A warehouse at midnight, handheld, following a man who is performing for a camera he has never met — the camera is a guest at a party it is also responsible for.
Roll :: Digital, 6400 ISO, two LED panels, a smoke machine from a party store, a torch taped to a stand.
Costume :: KJ MARLO in a puffer jacket and sunglasses at night. Eight friends in matching hoodies. DUKE in a coat with a phone in each hand.
Environment :: A rented warehouse on the edge of the county: a concrete floor, a pallet of something covered in a tarp, a boom box, a car with its headlights on.
@@SCENES
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. WAREHOUSE — MIDNIGHT</h4>
      <p class="action">KJ MARLO, twenty-two, walks the length of the concrete floor toward the camera, rapping under his breath, then again with his chest out.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">KJ</p><p class="dline">How's it look?</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">Dark.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">KJ</p><p class="dline">Dark is the point.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">JUNE</p><p class="dline">There's dark and there's unlit. Right now you're unlit.</p></div>
      <p class="action">Duke appears at her shoulder with a phone in each hand.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">DUKE</p><p class="dline">Whatever it costs, we've got it. We've got six hundred dollars and a car.</p></div>
    </div>
<div class="scene">
      <h4 class="slug">INT. WAREHOUSE — CONTINUOUS</h4>
      <p class="action">June moves one panel. Then the other. KJ turns into the light and becomes, immediately, a person you would look at.</p>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">KJ</p><p class="paren">(to the friends)</p><p class="dline">See that? That's the difference. That's why we're paying her.</p></div>
      <div class="speech"><p class="cue">DUKE</p><p class="paren">(quietly, to June)</p><p class="dline">We're paying you four hundred and the rest is for the smoke machine.</p></div>

      <p class="action">He grins at her like she has just said something cool, and she realises she has.</p>
    </div>

TITLE: THE PROCESS SERVER

@@SCENES
